This study examines the interaction between timestamp matching and admission control through a reproducible discrete event model. Six policies are compared on eight synthetic workload families, using 40 independent seeds per family and shared traces across policies. The model separates presentation timestamps, callback arrival times, waiting time and compositor service time. A first admissible match policy combines a timestamp tolerance with a finite waiting budget and a single outstanding composition. At a 10 ms tolerance and 20 ms waiting budget, it produces jointly admissible outputs for 91.59% of primary frames under the specified jitter workload, compared with 51.30% for immediate latest frame selection and 70.23% for full budget waiting. A phase offset workload produces no admissible pairs at the same tolerance, demonstrating that buffering cannot replace capture synchronization. Historical iPhone 16 Pro records for an adjacent screen recording workflow document camera interruption in a standard build and continued capture in a diagnostic build. These observations motivate treating source availability separately from pairing. The policy comparisons and timing metrics come from simulation.
