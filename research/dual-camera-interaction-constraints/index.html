Translating situated needs into interaction constraints for dual camera recording

A method for turning recording goals into testable rules for capture, local saving and export in a dual-camera interface.

Journal
Interacting with Computers
Author
Zishuo Yang

Research question

When a moment cannot be repeated, what evidence should a camera interface have before telling someone that a recording is ready?

Method

Two constructed scenarios, unboxing and a concert, inform a six-field requirement record covering source, situation, goal, behaviour, technical boundary and verification evidence. A finite-state model distinguishes capture, retention and export. Five selected engineering episodes contextualise the model’s assumptions.

Key findings

Reachable states · local goal
10
Reachable states · export goal
14
Injected transition faults detected
4
  • The reference model reached 10 states for the local goal and 14 for the export goal. No violation of the three specified invariants was found within the model.
  • Four deliberately introduced faults exposed early recording feedback, premature completion, false export success and loss of the retained local file.

Limitations

The scenarios are constructed, and the model abstracts away concurrency, timing and later file corruption. The retrospective records do not estimate user preference or deployed reliability.
